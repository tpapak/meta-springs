IPD: every patient is a spring

Two trials share individual participant data: Study 1 (A vs B) and Study 2 (A vs C). Fixed effects: η = μj + β1(x−x̄) + dk + β2(x−x̄), with dA = 0 and no interaction on A (multinma notation). The vertical axis in each study row is the patient covariate x. Each patient is one spring at their own height, from the study rod (tilted by the prognostic slope β1) to their treatment rod (tilted by the interaction β2).
× 1.00
Horizontal = linear predictor η (log-odds for binary, outcome scale for continuous). A patient's spring runs from the study rod (at −μj) to the treatment rod (at dk) at the patient's height, so its length is that patient's η. Tilts pivot at x̄; dashed lines show where each rod would stand untilted. Binary: responders push η up, non-responders push it down; a single patient has no rest length. Continuous: each patient is a Hooke spring whose rest length is their outcome y (dot); orange springs are compressed (y > η) and push the rods apart, blue springs are stretched (y < η) and pull them together. Summed into arm springs keeps only arm totals (no covariate): the patients of an arm add up to one spring with the same equilibrium. The spread slider stretches every patient's distance from the study's covariate mean: longer levers, smaller slope SEs.
