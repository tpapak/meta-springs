Nothing forces a single heterogeneity for the whole network. If the arms split into $G$ groups — say by treatment class — each group gets its own chain-length variable $N_g$ and its own $\tau^2_g$. Each arm in group g has variance $\tau_g^2/2$. A contrast within that group therefore has heterogeneity variance $\tau_g^2$; a contrast between groups g and h has variance $(\tau_g^2+\tau_h^2)/2$. Setting every $\tau_g^2=\tau^2$ recovers the usual common-heterogeneity model. Summing over every combination of chain lengths is a multi-species grand canonical sum, one boson species per group:

$$ \Xi \;=\; \sum_{N_1}\cdots\sum_{N_G} \; Z\bigl(\tau^2_1(N_1),\dots,\tau^2_G(N_G)\bigr), $$

and marginalising $\Xi$ over all species but one gives the posterior of that group's $\tau^2_g$ — a separate heterogeneity posterior per group, from the same machinery. In the animation the two species are $\{A,B\}$ and $\{C,D\}$; each arm's effective spring uses its own group's $\tau^2/2$. Each panel shows a conditional posterior slice, computed by sweeping the partition function over its $\tau^2$ with the other held fixed.

Which groupings are legitimate

The springs set the rule: because the $\tau$-springs are attached per arm and are exchangeable within a group (the next page), any partition of the arms into exchangeable blocks is a valid species decomposition — a single shared $\tau^2$ ($G=1$), class-specific $\tau^2$ — while orderings that would break exchangeability inside a block are not. The cost is a grid per species, so in practice $G$ is small; the two- and three-species cases cover the usual “does heterogeneity differ across classes?” question. When the lattice is too dense to enumerate, the outer sum can be replaced by MCMC on the discrete chain-length vector, with the deterministic inner Laplace supplying exact conditional effect posteriors.

FAQ
Multiple heterogeneity variances (comparison- or class-specific τ²)?

A multi-species grand-canonical sum: partition the arms into exchangeable groups, give each its own chain-length variable, sum the lattice, and marginalise over the other species to get each group’s $\tau^2$ posterior. The legitimate partitions are exactly those that keep the arm $\tau$-springs exchangeable within a group. See Part 2b.
