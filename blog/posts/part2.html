Grand canonical: the Bayesian solver

Thodoris Papakonstantinou

Part 1 left us with a single $\hat\tau^2$ (spring-REML) and the random-effects estimate at that value. A Bayesian wants the whole posterior — of $\tau^2$ and of every contrast. meta-springs gets it deterministically, by turning the $\tau$-spring into a grand canonical ensemble. Part 2 continues on the same 4-treatment running example.

Standard NMA convention: yij = μi + dj + ζij + ε, so positive ζij pulls the tau node toward larger outcome values. Each study has one y-row per arm; Si is a vertical orange bar at the centroid of its arm-treatment columns spanning those rows, so each τ-spring is horizontal at the same y as the corresponding part of Si. Black dots = tau (BLUP) nodes at x = xSi + scale·ζij; τ-chain coloured by chain length N (blue→cyan→amber→red). Obs springs are horizontal, tau node → treatment column. Right panel = posterior on τ² (flat prior); bars ∝ exp(ln Zk − max).
Why some BLUPs may cross the centroid: ζij = ((τ²/2)/(vij+τ²/2))·(yij − μ̂i − dj), so the side a tau node ends up on is dictated by the residual of the data against the network MAP, not by which side the treatment column sits on. With τ²>0, having every BLUP land on its "natural" side requires every within-study contrast to agree with the network estimate — which contradicts heterogeneity. Crossings are therefore an inherent feature of inconsistent data: the model is honestly absorbing the inconsistency into ζij.

In this part

  1. $\Xi$ gives the posterior Chain length = heterogeneity; $\Xi = \sum_N Z$; the $\tau^2$ posterior and a mixture-of-Gaussians for every effect; any prior for free; agreement & speed vs MCMC.
  2. Multiple $\tau^2$ posteriors One boson species per exchangeable group; marginalising the multi-species sum gives a separate heterogeneity posterior per group.
  3. Why the $\tau$-springs are exchangeable One i.i.d. $\tau$-spring length $\zeta_{ij}$ per arm on a shared study anchor; contrasts $\delta=\zeta-\zeta$ give the Lu–Ades $(I+J)/2$ correlation for free; indistinguishable elementary springs.
FAQ
What’s genuinely new versus GLS effects + profile-likelihood τ² + quadrature?

Each ingredient has a pedigree — deliberately; it’s why the answers match netmeta/metafor/bayesmeta. The contribution is the unification: one mechanical object giving the consistency effects, a deterministic full posterior of $\tau^2$ and every contrast for both Gaussian and exact-binomial NMA, grouped heterogeneity and FE-as-a-grid-point from the grand-canonical view, and SSPIs and meta-regression from the same compliances — fast (11–39× vs multinma here), reproducible to machine precision, with no convergence diagnostics, shipped as a gemtc-shaped drop-in.

How was it validated?

Effects and REML $\tau^2$ against netmeta and metafor (exact on curated sets; within 10% across 149/163 non-boundary nmadb networks); full posteriors against multinma (exact-binomial MCMC) and gemtc; the pairwise analytic posterior against bayesmeta ($\tau^2$ median to 3 s.f.).
