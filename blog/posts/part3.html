Network meta-regression

Thodoris Papakonstantinou

Parts 1 and 2 gave treatment effects and uncertainty from a spring network. Meta-regression asks how those effects change with a study characteristic.

Start with a supplied covariate: adding slope coordinates preserves the quadratic Gaussian energy. Then couple those slopes to share information across treatments. Finally, let the covariate be the latent study baseline: that makes the model nonlinear and introduces a local approximation in the current solver. Page 3b moves to individual participant data: every patient is a spring, study rows gain height, study rods tilt by the prognostic slope, and the within-study spread of the covariate identifies the slopes. Page 3c combines IPD with aggregate data (ML-NMR): an aggregate arm becomes one spring averaged over its covariate distribution.

The walkthrough connects the animation to the Haskell and Rust source, and includes commands for a Gaussian example and a separate binary baseline-risk example. It distinguishes analytic conditional calculations, numerical integration, and the approximations used for nonlinear regression.

Rods = treatments, glued through every study row: the rod's horizontal position at height x is the effect dj + β2,j·(x−x̄); the open ring marks dj at the pivot x̄, and the faint dashed line is where the rod would stand untilted. Within a study the covariate is constant, so the rod meets that study's arms at one position (the thick contact). Reference A is pinned (dA=0) and stays vertical. The rods are rigid: tilting a rod moves its contact at height xi by (xi−x̄) times the change in slope. The horizontal gap between the dashed line and the rod at a study's height is β2·(xi−x̄): the slope converts height into effect. No covariate sets every slope to zero (vertical rods, one network); Shared tilts B, C, D together; Unrelated tilts each rod by its own β2,t. Raise τ² and the BLUP nodes slide out along the τ-chains while the obs springs relax — τ² is now residual heterogeneity after regression on the covariate. The displayed τ² is contrast-scale; each arm carries variance τ²/2.

The four-treatment network with a fixed study covariate. Switch slope structures and vary $\tau^2$ to see adjusted effects and residual heterogeneity. The slope is fitted automatically.

In this part

  1. Covariates, slopes and the solver Fixed covariates extend the quadratic energy; exchangeable slopes add springs; latent baseline risk makes the model nonlinear. A runnable example connects the picture to the code.
  2. IPD: every patient a spring With individual participant data each patient is a spring. Without covariates the patients of an arm add up exactly to the arm spring; with a patient covariate they sit at different heights, and the within-study spread is the lever that identifies the slopes. The equilibrium is the glm() fit.
  3. ML-NMR: study rods and averaged springs Patient-level covariates give each study a height; study rods tilt by $\beta_1$, treatment rods by $\beta_2$; an aggregate arm is one spring with extent, and averaging its force law removes aggregation bias.
