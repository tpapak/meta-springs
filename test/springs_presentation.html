What it is — a spring network, solved in closed form

In statistical terms: GC does semi-analytic Bayesian random-effects meta-analysis by integrating out only the variance components — the heterogeneity τ² (and, for meta-regression, the between-study slope variance σ_B²) — computing their marginal posterior by quadrature on a grid, exactly the way bayesmeta (Röver) marginalises a single τ. Conditional on each grid value the treatment effects are multivariate normal (exact under a normal likelihood, Laplace-approximated for binomial data), so the full posterior is a grid-weighted mixture of conditional Gaussians — giving effects, league tables, contrasts and rank probabilities with no MCMC.

Every meta-analysis is a network of springs: studies and treatment contrasts are nodes; data and heterogeneity are springs pulling them into balance.

  • The MLE is the force-balance equilibrium (verified, |net force| ≈ 1e-12).
  • The posterior is the Grand-Canonical partition function over the variance components τ² (and σ_B²) — a Gaussian integral, evaluated on a grid.
  • No MCMC: no burn-in, no thinning, no R-hat to chase, fully reproducible.

One fit, every prior — free sensitivity analysis

The expensive part — Z(τ²) on the grid — is prior-independent. A prior is just a reweighting π(τ²) of the same grid, so N priors come from one fit.

MetaInsight runs a single default prior; with MCMC a sensitivity analysis means re-running from scratch for each.
